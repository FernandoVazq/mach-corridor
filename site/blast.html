Why the same blast is loud one day and quiet the next Inversions and wind bend sound back to the ground.

Sound bends toward slower air. On a sunny afternoon the air cools with height and a blast's noise curves up and away. On a clear, calm morning or evening, warm air sits on cool air near the ground and the noise curves back down, often several kilometres away. Wind does the same toward the side it blows to. Blasting codes and guidance (US Bureau of Mines RI 8485, mining regulators) say the same: avoid inversions and wind toward neighbours.
