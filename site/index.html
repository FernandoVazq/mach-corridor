How it works

Mach cutoff. Sound travels faster in warm air. A boom made high up, in cold air, bends upward as it descends into the warmer layers below. If some layer under the aircraft has an effective sound speed (sound speed + wind along the track) at least as fast as the aircraft's ground speed, the boom turns around before reaching the ground.

Mmax = ( maxground ≤ z ≤ za [ c(z) + w(z) ] − w(za) ) / c(za)

Each map pixel solves that equation on a full NOAA GFS profile (23 pressure levels + surface), every 6 hours out to 48 h, for 6 flight levels and 12 directions. Layers below the terrain are removed, so high plateaus get less margin.

Check: a standard atmosphere gives M 1.15 at FL360–400, matching Boom XB-1's boomless runs (M 1.12–1.18, 2025).

Not included (yet): forecast uncertainty (use the safety-margin slider), aircraft-specific near-field shape, humidity (<0.5% on c), horizontal gradients along the ray path, turns/accelerations. Research prototype — not for flight planning.

Reading the map. “Any direction” is the worst case across 12 headings; “Best” is the most favorable. Strong tailwinds aloft erase the margin; headwinds aloft expand it.
