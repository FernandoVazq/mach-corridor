How it works

Mach cutoff. Sound travels faster in warm air. A boom made high up in cold air bends upward as it descends into warmer layers. If some layer under the aircraft has an effective sound speed (sound + wind along the ray) at least as fast as the aircraft, the boom turns around before reaching the ground.

Mα = ( maxzf ≤ z < za [ c + w·eα ] − wa·eα ) / ( ca cos α ),  M = min over rays α

The turning point is a caustic (focused boom), so v2 requires it to sit at least 1 km above the highest terrain under the boom footprint (zf). Lateral rays (α ≠ 0) with crosswinds are included, the speed of sound uses virtual temperature, and the map subtracts a provisional forecast-error margin (default 0.03).

Data: NOAA GFS 1° pressure levels + 0.25° orography, 0–48 h every 6 h, updated 4× daily. 6 flight levels × 36 directions.

Sanity check (not validation): a standard atmosphere gives M 1.15 at FL400 with no buffer (M 1.14 with 1 km), in the range Boom XB-1 flew boomless (M 1.12–1.18, 2025).

Not included: overpressure (psf) and secondary booms, horizontal gradients along the ~40 km ray path, climb/descent angle (−3° costs ≈0.03 M), aircraft-specific near field. Global map shown for physics only; the FAA NPRM (docket FAA-2026-6935) covers the US, and other countries keep overland bans.
