How this was measured, and what it leaves out

  • Weather: NOAA GFS 1° analyses (taken as what actually happened) and 24-hour forecasts issued the day before, at 00 UTC every 7 days for a year. Boomless Mach from the same physics as the live map: rays down and to the sides, temperature, humidity and wind at every level, terrain under the boom plus a 1 km buffer.
  • Policies over land, territorial sea and the 24 nm boom carpet: today's ban (M0.95), one fixed Mach, a static chart (a given percentile of each spot's year), the forecast minus a margin, and perfect knowledge. Open water at M1.7; 150 nm subsonic climb and descent.
  • Aircraft: Overture-class (65 seats, 80% full, M1.7 design), fuel per nm from its efficiency curve; non-fuel cost $8,700 per block hour.
  • Routes are great circles flown every 10 nm, not the optimised routes of the app; acceleration and constant-Mach blocks are left out, so minutes saved are a little optimistic for short corridors.
  • The analysis is not the real atmosphere. The margins chart adds the missing error (0.012 Mach, correlated over 300 nm) measured against a year of weather balloons; the headline boom figures use it.
  • The 1 km buffer and the 1° grid are not yet validated against real flights (XB-1, NASA FaINT).
